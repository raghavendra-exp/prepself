Module 1 • Deep Time to Classical Antiquity

Ancient & Archaeological Heritage of Uttar Pradesh

1. Prehistoric Sequence in the Belan Valley (Prayagraj & Mirzapur)

Palaeolithic, Mesolithic & Neolithic Stratigraphy

Archaeology • Belan Valley

The Belan Valley (tributary of Tons/Ganga across Prayagraj, Mirzapur, and Sonbhadra districts) provides an uninterrupted stratigraphic sequence of human culture from the Lower Palaeolithic to the Neolithic period:

  • Lower & Middle Palaeolithic: Pebble tools, Acheulian handaxes, and cleavers found in gravel beds at Chopani Mando and Sihawal. Middle Palaeolithic flaked scrapers and points reflect transition to advanced hunting.
  • Mesolithic Settlements (Pratapgarh District): Sarai Nahar Rai, Mahadaha, and Damdama. Yielded microlithic blade industries, geometric bone ornaments, and human burials aligned west-to-east with grave goods (animal bones, shell beads). Damdama yielded 41 human burials, including double burials and female ornaments.
  • Neolithic Agriculture at Koldihwa: Situated on the Belan river, Koldihwa yielded circular wattle-and-daub huts, polished stone celts, handmade cord-impressed pottery, and charred grains of domesticated rice dating to the transition to sedentary village farming.
UPPSC Prelims Pointer: Sarai Nahar Rai is India's earliest recorded Mesolithic site with evidence of collective warfare/violent trauma on human skeletons and permanent geometric microlithic tool manufacture.

2. Lahuradewa (Sant Kabir Nagar) — Antiquity of Rice Domestication

Global Agrarian Revolution in the Middle Ganga Plain

Global Benchmark • c. 7000–6000 BCE

Excavations by the UP State Archaeology Department at Lahuradewa on the banks of a palaeo-lake in Sant Kabir Nagar district yielded revolutionary evidence that restructured global agricultural historiography:

  • Calibrated Radiocarbon Chronology: Calibrated AMS dating of carbonized rice grains and plant residues dates Period IA human occupation to c. 7000–6000 BCE.
  • Phytolith & Microbotanical Proof: Microscopic morphological analysis of bulliform phytoliths established conclusive evidence of fully domesticated wetland rice (Oryza sativa) alongside aquatic diatoms.
  • Cord-Impressed Ware Pottery: Handmade, low-fired cord-impressed ceramics with straw and rice husk tempering establish that ceramic technology developed synchronously with food production in eastern Uttar Pradesh.
  • Historiographical Impact: Decisively dismantled the colonial theory that rice agriculture diffused into India exclusively from China's Yangtze River Valley, demonstrating that the Middle Ganga plain was an autonomous global cradle of agricultural civilization.

3. The Sanauli Excavations (Baghpat) & Indus Frontiers (Alamgirpur)

Bronze Age War Chariots, Royal Sarcophagi & OCP Continuum

ASI Landmark • Baghpat & Meerut

Uttar Pradesh formed the crucial eastern boundary and late transition zone of the Bronze Age Indus-Saraswati Civilization:

  • Alamgirpur (Meerut): Situated on the Hindon River (tributary of Yamuna), Alamgirpur marks the easternmost boundary of the Mature Harappan Civilization. Yielded typical Harappan black-on-red painted pottery, terracotta triangular cakes, cart frames, and faience beads.
  • Sanauli (Baghpat District): Landmark Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) excavations directed by Dr. S.K. Manjul (2018–2021) unearthing an elite necropolis dating to c. 2000–1800 BCE:
    • Solid-Disk Wheeled War Chariots: Three two-wheeled wooden chariots reinforced with copper strips, triangular copper plaques, and draught-pole fittings, driven by animals.
    • Royal Sarcophagus Burials: Eight anthropomorphic wooden coffins decorated with copper inlays and horned headdress motifs.
    • Martial Metallurgy: Antennae-hilted copper swords, leaf-shaped daggers, copper helmets, composite wooden shields, and steatite bead necklaces, proving an organized indigenous warrior aristocracy in the Upper Yamuna-Ganga Doab.
Historical Significance: Sanauli firmly connects the Late Harappan Phase, Ochre Coloured Pottery (OCP), and the Copper Hoard Culture of Western UP with early Epic and Vedic martial traditions.

4. The 8 Mahajanapadas in Uttar Pradesh (c. 6th Century BCE)

The Political Epicenter of Second Urbanization in Madhyadesha

Anguttara Nikaya • 8 of 16 States

Out of the sixteen great territorial states (Mahajanapadas) mentioned in the Buddhist Anguttara Nikaya and Jain Bhagavati Sutra, exactly eight were situated within modern Uttar Pradesh:

Mahajanapada Capital(s) Modern UP Districts Polity Type Key Historical Rulers & Events

5. Sacred Geography of Heterodox Faiths in UP

Buddhism & Jainism in the Gangetic Heartland

Sarnath • Kushinagar • Mathura

☸️ Buddhist Sacred Geography

  • Sarnath (Rishipattana / Deer Park): First sermon (Dhammachakkapavattana) to the Panchavaggiya monks. Dhamek Stupa and Ashokan Lion Capital.
  • Shravasti: Buddha spent 25 monsoonal retreats (Varshavas) at Jetavana; performed the Twin Miracles (Yamakapratiharya); converted Angulimala.
  • Kushinagar: Mahaparinirvana under the twin Sala trees; Nirvana Temple and Ramabhar Stupa (cremation ground).
  • Sankisa (Farrukhabad): Legend of Buddha descending from Trayastrimsa Heaven accompanied by Indra and Brahma; Ashokan elephant capital.
  • Piprahwa (Siddharthnagar): Excavation of the ancient Kapilavastu stupa containing steatite relic caskets with early Brahmi inscriptions confirming Buddha's Shakya bone relics.

🪷 Jain Sacred Heritage

  • Ayodhya: Birthplace of 5 Tirthankaras: Rishabhanatha (Adinatha), Ajitanatha, Abhinandananatha, Sumatinatha, and Anantanatha.
  • Varanasi: Birthplace of 7th Tirthankara Suparshvanatha and 23rd Tirthankara Parshvanatha (Bhelupur).
  • Shravasti: Birthplace of 3rd Tirthankara Sambhavanatha.
  • Kaushambi: Birthplace of 6th Tirthankara Padmaprabha.
  • Mathura (Kankali Tila): Epicenter of ancient Jain plastic art; excavated Svetambara Jain stupas, Ayagapattas (votive tablets of homage), and inscribed Tirthankara images from Shunga and Kushana periods.

6. Classical Imperial Monuments in UP (Mauryas, Kushanas & Guptas)

Sarnath Lion Capital, Mathura Art, Bhitargaon & Deogarh

Architecture • Art Schools
  • Mauryan Pillars & Edicts: Sarnath Lion Capital (National Emblem adopted 26 Jan 1950, four Asiatic lions, Dharmachakra with 24 spokes, bull, horse, elephant, lion). Ashokan pillar edicts at Prayagraj (Kosambi-Allahabad pillar), Meerut (later shifted to Delhi by Firoz Shah Tughlaq), and Minor Rock Edict at Ahraura (Mirzapur).
  • Mathura School of Art (Kushana Period): Flourished using spotted red sandstone (Sikri sandstone); produced the earliest indigenous anthropomorphic images of the Buddha with smiling countenance, fleshy torso, open eyes, and transparent sanghati; created the iconic headless statue of Emperor Kanishka at Mat (Mathura).
  • Bhitargaon Brick Temple (Kanpur): 5th-century Gupta masterpiece; oldest surviving complete terracotta-brick temple in India; features a true arched sanctum ceiling, recessed exterior panels with terracotta sculptures of Shiva, Vishnu, and river goddesses.
  • Deogarh Dashavatara Temple (Lalitpur): Classic Gupta Panchayatana temple in stone; iconic high-relief sculptural panel of Sheshashayi Vishnu reclining on serpent Ananta with Lakshmi massaging his feet; Nar-Narayana and Gajendramoksha panels.
  • Bhitari Pillar Inscription (Ghazipur): Sanskrit inscription of Skandagupta recording his triumphant defeat of the invading White Hunas (Hephthalites), preserving northern India from nomadic devastation.
  • Harshavardhana at Kannauj & Prayag: Shifted imperial capital from Thanesar to Kannauj (Kanyakubja/Mahodaya). Convened the celebrated 643 CE Kannauj Religious Assembly (presided over by Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang) and the quinquennial Prayag Mahamokshaparishad where he distributed the entire royal treasury in charity.
Module 2 • Sultanate, Mughals & Awadh

Medieval History & Courtly Traditions of Uttar Pradesh

1. Jaunpur Sharqi Sultanate — "Shiraz of the East" (1394–1479 CE)

Independent Muslim Polity, Propylon Architecture & Cultural Renaissance

Jaunpur • Sharqi Dynasty

Founded along the Gomti River by Malik Sarwar (Sultanu-sh-Sharq, governor under Firoz Shah Tughlaq), Jaunpur became an independent regional sultanate and a radiant beacon of Islamic scholarship, music, and distinct architecture:

  • Shiraz-i-Hind (Shiraz of the East): Term coined by Mughal emperor Shah Jahan to honor Jaunpur's status as a pre-eminent seat of learning, theology, and Persian poetry, attracting scholars from across Persia and Central Asia.
  • Atala Masjid (Completed 1408 CE by Sultan Ibrahim Shah Sharqi): The masterpiece of Sharqi architecture. Characterized by a gigantic 75-foot sloping central pylon (propylon screen) adorned with recessed arches, absence of conventional minarets, squinch-supported domes, and harmonic integration of Hindu trabeate lintels with Islamic arcuate vaults.
  • Other Monuments: Jama Masjid (Husain Shah Sharqi), Lal Darwaza Masjid (Bibi Raji), and the celebrated Shahi Bridge (Akbari Bridge) built over the Gomti by Munim Khan under Akbar.
  • Musical Innovations: Sultan Husain Shah Sharqi was a celebrated composer of classical music, credited with formulating Khayal melodies and developing Ragas such as Jaunpuri Todi and Husaini Kanada.

2. Mughal Heartland: Agra & Fatehpur Sikri

Imperial Capitals, Charbagh Gardens & World Heritage Architecture

UNESCO Heritage • Agra & Fatehpur Sikri
  • Agra's Imperial Trajectory: Founded by Sikandar Lodi in 1504 as military base against Rajputana. Babur laid India's first geometric Charbagh garden (Aram Bagh / Ram Bagh) on the Yamuna. Akbar constructed the formidable red sandstone Agra Fort (Jahangiri Mahal, Delhi Gate). Shah Jahan transformed it with white marble (Diwan-i-Khas, Moti Masjid, Sheesh Mahal) and built the Taj Mahal (1631–1648, chief architect Ustad Ahmad Lahori).
  • Fatehpur Sikri (1571–1585 CE): Akbar's planned capital built in red sandstone on Sikri ridge to venerate Sufi saint Sheikh Salim Chishti:
    • Buland Darwaza: 54-meter monumental gateway erected in 1601 to commemorate Akbar's conquest of Gujarat.
    • Tomb of Salim Chishti: Pristine white marble mausoleum enclosed by intricate serpentine brackets and hand-carved pierced jali screens.
    • Diwan-i-Khas: Features a massive central octagonal pillar with 36 radiating serpentine brackets supporting a circular elevated platform for theological consultations.
    • Panch Mahal: Five-tiered open columnar pavilion inspired by Buddhist Vihara structures for ventilation and royal recreation.
    • Ibadat Khana (1575): House of Worship where Akbar debated religious theology with Sunni scholars, Sufis, Shias, Brahmins (Purushottam, Devi), Jains (Hira Vijaya Suri), Zoroastrians (Meherji Rana), and Jesuits (Aquaviva).
  • Kalinjar Fort (Banda District): Ancient Bundelkhand hill fort where Emperor Sher Shah Suri met his death in May 1545 during a siege when a cannon rocket (Huqqa) rebounded against the fortress ramparts and ignited the gunpowder magazine.
  • Itmad-ud-Daulah's Tomb (Agra): Commissioned by Empress Nur Jahan for her father Mirza Ghiyas Beg (1628); known as the "Jewel Box" and precursor to the Taj Mahal; marked the first extensive architectural deployment of Pietra Dura (parchin kari) inlay on marble.

3. Nawabs of Awadh & Composite Ganga-Jamuni Tehzeeb (1722–1856)

From Faizabad to Lucknow • Baroque Lakhori Architecture & Secular Ethos

Awadh • Ganga-Jamuni Tehzeeb

The Nawabs of Awadh established a culturally vibrant successor state that fostered the celebrated Ganga-Jamuni Tehzeeb (syncretic Hindu-Muslim cultural ethos):

  • Dynastic Lineage: Saadat Khan Burhan-ul-Mulk (1722) -> Safdarjung -> Shuja-ud-Daula (defeated at Battle of Buxar 1764; signed Treaty of Allahabad 1765 granting Diwani of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa to Clive) -> Asaf-ud-Daula (shifted capital from Faizabad to Lucknow in 1775).
  • Bara Imambara & Bhool Bhulaiya / Bhulbhulaiya (1784): Commissioned by Asaf-ud-Daula as a public famine-relief project under architect Kifayatullah. Features the world's largest unsupported vaulted hall (50m x 16m x 15m) constructed exclusively of interlocking thin Lakhori bricks and lime mortar without a single pillar or iron girder; houses the acoustic, multi-level Bhool Bhulaiya (Bhulbhulaiya) maze.
  • Rumi Darwaza: 60-foot monumental gateway built in 1784 along the Bara Imambara, patterned after the Sublime Porte (Bab-i-Humayun) of Istanbul.
  • Chhota Imambara (Imambara Husainabad): Built by Muhammad Ali Shah (1838) with exquisite Belgian glass chandeliers, gold-plated dome, and calligraphy.
  • Nawab Wajid Ali Shah (1847–1856): The last ruling Nawab of Awadh; scholar-poet under pen-name 'Akhtarpiya', maestro of classical Kathak dance under guru Thakur Prasad, composer of iconic Thumris ("Babul mora naihar chhooto hi jaaye"), and creator of Rahas dance ballets; deposed and exiled to Metiabruz (Calcutta) by Lord Dalhousie in 1856 on charges of "maladministration" under James Outram's report.

1. The Great Revolt of 1857 in Uttar Pradesh (The Main Epicenter)

From Meerut Outbreak to Awadh Rural Resistance

10 May 1857 • Sepoys, Taluqdars & Peasants

Uttar Pradesh formed the supreme storm-center of the 1857 rebellion, where military mutiny transformed into an intense, universal agrarian war against East India Company rule:

Center in UP Indian Leaders / Martyrs British Suppressors Key Tactical & Historical Dynamics
Meerut Sepoys of 3rd Native Cavalry; Kadam Singh General Hewitt 10 May 1857: Sepoys break military jail, eliminate British officers, and march across the Yamuna to Delhi, proclaiming Bahadur Shah Zafar Emperor of Hindustan.
Lucknow Begum Hazrat Mahal, Birjis Qadr, Maulvi Ahmadullah Shah Sir Henry Lawrence, Outram, Havelock, Colin Campbell Battle of Chinhat victory; Siege of Lucknow Residency (Lawrence killed). Mass participation of Awadh taluqdars and sepoys. Recaptured by Campbell in March 1858.
Kanpur Nana Sahib (Dhondu Pant), Tatya Tope, Azimullah Khan Sir Hugh Wheeler, Havelock, Colin Campbell Siege of Wheeler's entrenchment; Sati Chaura Ghat and Bibighar incidents. Nana Sahib fled to Nepal; Tatya Tope launched guerrilla warfare across Bundelkhand.
Jhansi Rani Lakshmibai, Gangadhar Rao's followers Sir Hugh Rose Resisted annexation under Doctrine of Lapse. Fought heroically against Hugh Rose ("The bravest of the rebel leaders"). Joined Tatya Tope at Kalpi, martyred at Kotah-ki-Serai (Gwalior) on 18 June 1858.
Bareilly (Rohilkhand) Khan Bahadur Khan (grandson of Hafiz Rahmat Khan) Sir Colin Campbell Proclaimed Nawab Nazim, established independent revenue administration across Rohilkhand; organized large volunteer army before capture and execution.

Baraut (Baghpat) Shah Mal Dunlop (Khakee Risala) Mobilized 84 Jat villages (Chaurasi des); organized secret intelligence network; cut off British supply lines to Delhi ridge before martyrdom in July 1857.
Mathura & Bijnor Devi Singh (Mathura), Mohammad Khan (Bijnor) British District Magistrates Peasant mobilization against moneylenders and colonial revenue record offices.

2. Socio-Religious & Educational Resurgence in UP

Aligarh, Deoband, BHU & Radhasoami Movements

Reform & Revival • 19th Century
  • Aligarh Movement (Sir Syed Ahmed Khan): Established the Scientific Society (1864 at Ghazipur, later Aligarh) to translate Western scientific works; founded the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental (MAO) College at Aligarh in 1875 (upgraded to Aligarh Muslim University in 1920); launched the journal Tahzib-ul-Akhlaq (Social Reformer) promoting rationalism, scientific inquiry, and modern Western education among Muslims.
  • Deoband Movement (1866): Founded at Saharanpur by Islamic scholars Muhammad Qasim Nanautavi and Rashid Ahmad Gangohi (Darul Uloom Deoband). An orthodox revivalist school aimed at propagating unadulterated Quranic and Hadith teachings, strictly opposed to British imperialism; rejected Sir Syed's pro-British political stance; championed Indian National Congress nationalism through Jamiat Ulema-e-Hind.
  • Bharat Dharma Mahamandala & Pt. Madan Mohan Malaviya: Formed to organize orthodox Sanatan Dharma educational defense; culminated in Malaviya's foundation of Banaras Hindu University (BHU) at Varanasi in 1916 with Annie Besant's Central Hindu College as its institutional nucleus.
  • Radhasoami Movement (Agra, 1861): Founded by banker Shiv Dayal Singh (Soamiji Maharaj) in Agra; emphasized Surat Shabd Yoga, meditation on the inner divine sound, equality of all castes, and community living without ascetic renunciation.

3. Peasant Struggles & Revolutionary Martyrdom in UP

Kisan Sabhas, Chauri Chaura, HRA, Kakori Martyrs & Ballia Government

Mass Awakening • 1920–1942
  • Peasant Mobilization (Kisan Sabhas):
    • Oudh Kisan Sabha (October 1920): Formed at Pratapgarh by Baba Ramchandra (wandering sanyasi reciting Tulsidas Ramcharitmanas to organize tenants), Jawaharlal Nehru, and Gaurishankar Mishra; fought against Bedakhli (arbitrary eviction) and Nazrana.
    • Eka Movement (1921–1922): Radical peasant revolt in Hardoi, Bahraich, Sitapur, and Barabanki under Madari Pasi; peasants took religious oaths around ritual fire-pits to pay only recorded rent, refuse Begar (unpaid forced labor), and reject land eviction orders.
    • All India Kisan Sabha (11 April 1936): Formed at the Lucknow INC session with Swami Sahajanand Saraswati as President and N.G. Ranga as General Secretary; released the landmark Kisan Manifesto demanding abolition of Zamindari.
  • Chauri Chaura Incident (4 February 1922, Gorakhpur): Clashes between peaceful peasant demonstrators and colonial police escalated when police opened fire; outraged crowd surrounded and burned the police station, resulting in the deaths of 22 policemen. Mahatma Gandhi promptly suspended the pan-Indian Non-Cooperation Movement on 12 February 1922 (Bardoli resolution), citing lack of discipline in non-violence.
  • Revolutionary Phase II in UP:
    • Hindustan Republican Association (HRA): Founded at Kanpur in October 1924 by Sachindra Nath Sanyal, Ram Prasad Bismil, and Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee to overthrow British imperialism via armed revolution.
    • Kakori Train Action (9 August 1925): Revolutionaries stopped 8-Down passenger train near Kakori (Lucknow) and requisitioned British government treasury cash. Four iconic leaders were hanged: Ram Prasad Bismil (Gorakhpur Jail), Ashfaqullah Khan (Faizabad Jail), Roshan Singh (Naini Central Jail, Prayagraj), and Rajendra Lahiri (Gonda Jail).
    • HSRA & Chandrashekhar Azad: Reorganized at Feroz Shah Kotla (1928) adding "Socialist" to HRA. Chandrashekhar Azad ('Balraj') coordinated operations across UP until his martyrdom in a solitary gun battle against police at Alfred Park, Prayagraj, on 27 February 1931.
  • Quit India Movement & Ballia Parallel Government (August 1942): Chittu Pandey ("Sher-e-Ballia") established an independent parallel government (Prati Sarkar) in Ballia district, released all imprisoned political prisoners, and took administrative control before military suppression.
Module 4 • Performing Arts & GI Heritage

Living Cultural Traditions, Musical Gharanas & GI Crafts of UP

1. Classical Kathak Dance — Lucknow vs. Banaras Gharanas

From Temple Storytelling to Courtly Virtuosity

Classical Dance • Kathak

Kathak (derived from Sanskrit Katha, story) is the only classical dance style indigenous to northern India, preserved and elevated by hereditary families in Uttar Pradesh:

Aesthetic Feature Lucknow Gharana (Kalka-Bindadin Lineage) Banaras (Varanasi) Gharana (Janaki Prasad Lineage)
Origins & Patronage Founded by Ishwari Prasad; flourished under Nawab Wajid Ali Shah. Promoted by Thakur Prasad, Bindadin, Achchan Maharaj, Shambhu Maharaj, Birju Maharaj. Founded by Janaki Prasad; patronized by the Maharajas of Kashi and temple traditions of Varanasi. Exponents: Pt. Gopi Krishna, Sitara Devi.
Core Focus & Temperament Renowned for Nazakat (subtlety), Laya elegance, graceful torso movements, and emotional depth in facial mime (Bhava). Celebrated for vigorous footwork (Tatkar), powerful execution, robust rhythmic endurance, and unadorned precision.
Poetic Form for Abhinaya Dominance of Thumri, Dadra, and Ghazal verses composed by Bindadin Maharaj and Wajid Ali Shah ('Akhtarpiya'). Devotional Dhrupad, Padas, and Stotras celebrating Shiva (Tandava), Rama, and Krishna.
Rhythmic & Bol Structure Uses Tabla and Pakhawaj syllables; exquisite Gat Nikas and Gat Bhav (micro-stories depicted without words). Exclusive use of pure Natwari Bols (dance syllables like dha-dha-din-ta) rather than tabla bols; distinctive entry step (Uthan).

2. Classical Music Gharanas of Uttar Pradesh

Banaras, Kirana, Agra & Lucknow Vocal/Instrumental Traditions

Hindustani Classical Music
  • Banaras Gharana: Known for rich versatility across Thumri, Dadra, Chaiti, Kajri, and Tappa. Iconic vocalists: Girija Devi (Queen of Thumri), Rasoolan Bai, Siddheshwari Devi. Instrumental virtuosos: Shehnai maestro Bharat Ratna Ustad Bismillah Khan, Tabla giants Pt. Samta Prasad (Gudai Maharaj) and Pt. Kishan Maharaj.
  • Kirana Gharana (Shamli District): Founded by Ustad Abdul Karim Khan and Ustad Abdul Wahid Khan in village Kirana; characterized by slow, introspective tempo (Vilambit Khayal), precise Swara intonation (Shuddha Swara), and emotional melody; celebrated exponent: Bharat Ratna Pandit Bhimsen Joshi, Gangubai Hangal, Prabha Atre.
  • Agra Gharana: Traces lineage to Haji Sujan Khan (court of Akbar); celebrated for "Nauhar Bani" style; legendary doyen: Ustad Faiyyaz Khan ("Aftab-e-Mausiqi"); renowned for robust Nom-Tom Alap, Bol-Taans, and deep, resonant chest voice.
  • Lucknow Gharana (Tabla): Founded by Modu Khan and Bakshu Khan; introduced the "Khula Baaj" (open palm strikes) combining Pakhawaj resonant sounds with delicate multi-finger finger-work suited for Kathak dance and Khayal accompaniment.

3. Folk Traditions, Regional Music & UNESCO Theatre

Kajri, Alha, Nautanki & The Ramnagar Ramlila

Folk Heritage • Purvanchal & Bundelkhand

🎵 Folk Song Genres of UP

  • Kajri: Monsoon folk songs of Mirzapur and Varanasi; themes of Radha-Krishna romance, rain showers, and sweet sorrow of separation.
  • Alha-Udal: Heroic martial ballads of Bundelkhand; recounts the 52 battles of warrior brothers Alha and Udal against Prithviraj Chauhan.
  • Birha: Purvanchal Bhojpuri narrative singing portraying socio-historical tales, bravery, and contemporary issues.
  • Chaiti: Sung in the Hindi month of Chaitra (spring) across Varanasi and Purvanchal, invoking Lord Rama.
  • Rasiya & Hori: Braj region folk music celebrating Lord Krishna and Radha's Holi festivities in Mathura, Barsana, and Vrindavan.

🎭 Folk Theatre & Fairs

  • Ramnagar Ramlila (Varanasi): 31-day open-air environmental theatre performed across the town of Ramnagar without electrical amplification; inscribed on the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage List.
  • Nautanki: Popular operatic folk theatre; two distinct schools: Kanpur Style (social satire, direct dialogues) and Hathras Style (poetic, high-pitched operatic singing accompanied by Nagara drums).
  • Raslila: Traditional Krishna dance drama centered in Mathura and Vrindavan.
  • Kumbh Mela (Prayagraj): World's largest peaceful religious gathering at the Triveni Sangam; Mahakumbh celebrated every 12 years; inscribed by UNESCO.
  • Notable Fairs: Bateshwar Fair (Agra livestock fair on Yamuna), Deva Sharif (Barabanki), Nauchandi Mela (Meerut).

4. Geographical Indication (GI) Crafts & Handloom Traditions of UP

Exquisite Artisan Centers & Export Excellence

GI Registry • 15+ Heritage Crafts

Lucknow Chikan Craft & Zardozi

District: Lucknow

Delicate shadow needlework on fine muslin fabrics with 32 distinctive stitches (Tepchi, Bakhiya, Phanda, Murri, Jaali); paired with ornate Zardozi metallic gold/silver thread embroidery.

Moradabad Metal Craft ("Pital Nagari")

District: Moradabad

Hand-cast, etched, and engraved brassware, ceremonial lamps, vases, and sculptures; largest exporter of brass handicrafts in India.

Varanasi Brocades & Silk Sarees

District: Varanasi

Legendary Kinkhab handloom weaving using pure mulberry silk interlaced with gold and silver Zari threads; distinctive floral and Mughal kalga motifs.

Saharanpur Wood Carvings

District: Saharanpur

Intricate filigree and deep-relief carving on seasoned Sheesham and Teak wood, producing ornamental screens, furniture, and boxes.

Khurja Ceramic Pottery

District: Bulandshahr

Glazed, lead-free, hand-painted floral ceramics dating to Afghan and Timurid artisan lineages; traditional pottery cluster of national repute.

Kannauj Perfume (Attar)

District: Kannauj

Traditional hydro-distillation in copper stills (Deg-Bhapka process) extracting natural botanical essential oils and the famous Mitti Attar (baked clay rain fragrance).

Mirzapur & Bhadohi Handmade Carpets

Districts: Bhadohi & Mirzapur

Centuries-old Persian-style knotted woolen carpets (Kaleen) and Dhurries patronized since Akbar's court; largest carpet belt in Asia.

खण्ड 3: शासकीय पत्राचार (Patrachar / Official Drafting) — 24 अंक

3 Drafts × 8 Marks

अभ्यर्थियों को 3 भिन्न-भिन्न प्रशासनिक प्रारूपों में पत्र तैयार करने होते हैं। प्रत्येक पत्र का प्रारूप, प्रेषक-प्रेषिती का स्थान, विषय, संबोधन, स्वनिर्देश तथा हस्ताक्षर का स्थान निर्धारित प्रशासनिक नियमावली के अनुरूप होना चाहिए:

1. शासकीय पत्र (Official Letter):

एक सरकार से दूसरी सरकार, विभागाध्यक्षों अथवा सांविधिक संस्थाओं के मध्य पूर्णतः औपचारिक विनिमय। संबोधन: 'महोदय', स्वनिर्देश: 'भवदीय'। अन्य पुरुष (Third Person) शैली।

2. अर्ध-शासकीय पत्र (Demi-Official / D.O.):

समान पदस्थ अधिकारियों के बीच व्यक्तिगत ध्यान आकर्षण अथवा त्वरित निस्तारण हेतु। संबोधन: 'प्रिय श्री...', स्वनिर्देश: 'आपका / आपका शुभेच्छु'। प्रथम पुरुष (First Person) शैली।

3. कार्यालय ज्ञाप (Office Memorandum):

सचिवालय के विभिन्न विभागों अथवा अनुभागों के मध्य सूचनाओं के अंतर्विभागीय आदान-प्रदान हेतु। इसमें संबोधन ('महोदय') तथा स्वनिर्देश ('भवदीय') नहीं होता।

4. कार्यालय आदेश (Office Order):

कार्यालय के आंतरिक कर्मचारियों की नियुक्ति, स्थानांतरण, अवकाश स्वीकृति अथवा कार्य विभाजन संबंधी आंतरिक प्रशासनिक आदेश। भाषा सर्वथा आज्ञापरक होती है।

5. परिपत्र (Circular):

जब कोई एक ही शासकीय आदेश अथवा नीतिगत निर्देश एक साथ अनेक अधीनस्थ अधिकारियों अथवा सभी जनपदों को समान रूप से प्रेषित किया जाना हो (गश्ती पत्र)।

6. विज्ञप्ति / अधिसूचना (Notification):

नियम, उपनियम, वैधानिक शक्तियां, आयोगों के गठन अथवा गजटेड अधिकारियों की नियुक्ति को जनसाधारण के सूचनार्थ सरकारी राजपत्र (Gazette) में प्रकाशित करने का प्रारूप।
