🔬 How Normalization Works in Multi-Shift Exams

In exams conducted across multiple shifts (like SSC CGL, RRB NTPC, and IBPS), paper difficulty varies across shifts. Examination commissions employ standard deviation formulas or equi-percentile equating methods to ensure fair parity:

  • Tougher shifts receive score increments to compensate for higher question difficulty.
  • Easier shifts see moderation in raw marks to balance the cohort distribution.
  • Accuracy & variance play a vital role during tie-breaking and normalization score generation.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions: Percentile & Cutoffs

What is the difference between percentage and percentile in competitive exams? Percentage represents raw marks scored out of total marks (e.g. 75/100 = 75%). Percentile measures your relative standing against all test-takers (e.g. 99th percentile means you scored equal to or higher than 99% of candidates).
How is percentile calculated in national entrance exams? The standard formula is: ((Total Candidates - Candidate Rank) / Total Candidates) * 100. In CAT, JEE, and CUET, normalized session scores are converted into percentiles across shifts.
